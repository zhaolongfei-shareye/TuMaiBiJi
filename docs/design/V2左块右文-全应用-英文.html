V2 Block-left · full app, English copy

Same six colours, same ink pairings, same CSS motifs as the Chinese sheet and as the shipped V1 — only the copy language changes. The point of this version is to see what breaks first under English's longer words: the 88px block, the two-line title, and the chips row.

Three rules had to be added for English that Chinese does not need: the block label may now wrap to two lines, titles are clamped at two lines, and letter-spacing goes back to 0 (the -0.25px squeeze that suits CJK makes long Latin words collide).

Light · main flow

Four to five rows per screen, two-line summary each. Below the fold the density is the same as the Chinese sheet — the title just runs out of lines sooner.

Where the fade actually bites

Nothing is truncated in code and no name is rejected on input — the block just stops being readable where it runs out of room. The date line stays because it is the second thing the block has to say.

What the English pass actually exposed, in order of how much it costs to fix.

One — the block label is settled: one line, fade the tail. An 88px block holds about 9 bold characters, so "Product", "Reading" and "Unsorted" fit whole and a long name simply fades out at the block edge — no ellipsis, because a half word with "…" reads as a broken word, while a fade reads as "there is more, you don't need it". Nothing is lost: the colour carries recognition, the title on the right carries reading. The fade sits in the last 14% of a fixed-width box, so short names end before it and are untouched. Chinese names are 2–3 glyphs and never reach it, which makes this an English-only rule that costs nothing to ship.

Two — titles lose a line. The same sentence is 1.6–2× longer in Latin letters, so a two-line clamp in English holds about 11 words. Either the list shows one title line and no summary, or two title lines and the summary drops to one. The sheet above keeps two + two and lets long titles clamp, which is the honest middle.

Three — three places where English genuinely cannot be a translation. The 56px badge on Create ("链 / 图 / 写" is one character each; "URL" fits, "screenshot" does not), the "未分类" fallback wording, and the wallpaper names, which had to be re-invented rather than translated.

Four — the uppercase micro-labels only pay off in English. The 9.5px letter-spaced eyebrows (SUMMARY / KEY POINTS) do nothing in Chinese, because Chinese has no case. They are a free improvement to the English build and should not be copied back as a Latin-letter-sized hack into the Chinese one.
